## Apple's Business Model & Strategic Positioning

- **Customer value:** premium design, quality, ease of use, seamless cross-device experience
- **Monetization:** premium devices + accessories + recurring high-margin services from a large installed base
- **Operating model:** own design/software/IP + selected key technologies; outsource most assembly/logistics; orchestrate direct + partner channels

**Installed-base flywheel:** Premium devices → More active devices → Services + accessories (loyalty, switching costs, repeat purchases)

**Key nuance:** Apple is manufacturing-light, not asset-free. It outsources factories while retaining high-control, high-value activities and selectively internalizing strategic technologies.

## The Challenge Map (WHAT)

1. **Maturing smartphone market** — units plateau; margins/share under pressure
2. **Product proliferation** — more models + older SKUs → forecast/allocation error
3. **Supplier concentration** — core spend concentrated; ~12 key components often single-sourced
4. **China concentration** — >90% of Apple products assembled in China; Foxconn dominates iPhone volume
5. **Technology dependence** — external owners of critical tech affect cost, IP, differentiation, timing
6. **Business-model expansion** — wearables/services need different suppliers, processes, partner ecosystems

**Discuss:** Which challenge is a consequence of Apple's past success—and which is a true structural weakness?

## Retail Is Not "Downstream Only"

- Direct channels: **31%** (stores + online + direct sales)
- Indirect channels: **69%** (carriers + wholesalers + resellers)
- **510** Apple Stores in 25 countries

**Apple Stores create more than sales:** premium brand experience/product education · Genius Bar service/repair access · real-time sales/repair data for forecasting + quality issues · direct customer relationship strengthens ecosystem engagement

**Amazon agreement (2018):** Amazon became an authorized reseller and committed to remove non-authorized third-party Apple sellers.

> Strategic implication: Apple trades some direct-channel control for reach while preserving brand/channel governance.

**Technology control:** Cook Doctrine (own/control primary technologies) · Dialog + Intel acquisitions accelerated internal capability · vertical integration raises fixed/R&D cost but improves control

**Responsibility:** 1,049 supplier assessments in 45 countries (2018) · labor issues persisted despite monitoring · Trade-in + Daisy + recovered cobalt = closed-loop material flows

## SO WHAT — Competitive Advantage as a System

**Chain of capabilities:** Design → Supplier leverage → Flexible capacity → Demand sensing → Fast logistics → Channel control → Reverse logistics

Supporting capabilities: low inventory · launch coordination · quality discipline · information visibility · supplier financing

> **Competitive advantage = coordination + execution.** Apple can run low inventories because supplier capacity is flexible, launches are tightly orchestrated, information is fast, transport is fast, and suppliers help finance working capital. Copying one practice without the surrounding system can destroy performance.

## Two Frameworks Point to Segmentation

**Lens 1 — Demand × Supply uncertainty:** Efficient (low demand/low supply uncertainty) · Responsive (high demand/low supply uncertainty) · Risk-hedging (low demand/high supply uncertainty) · **Agile** (high demand/high supply uncertainty) — matches Apple at launch

**Lens 2 — Product maturity (Fisher):** Efficient supply chain MATCHES functional/mature products; Responsive supply chain MATCHES innovative/uncertain products; the crossed combinations are MISMATCHES.

> **Implication:** Do not redesign "the iPhone supply chain" as one block. Shift predictable, mature flows toward efficiency while preserving agility at launches, critical technologies, and risky nodes.

## Build Resilience Selectively (Criticality × Substitutability Matrix)

||Low criticality|High criticality|
|---|---|---|
|**Low substitutability (easy to replace)**|Standardize / market buy — commodity items; price competition, standards, capacity options|Strategic partnership — critical but replaceable; long-term capacity, co-investment, joint process improvement|
|**High substitutability (hard to replace)**|Buffer / qualify backup — difficult-to-replace but lower strategic impact; safety stock, tooling portability, qualified alternate|Own / dual-source / redundancy — critical + difficult to replace; vertical integration, dual source, geographic redundancy|

**Design principles:** don't diversify every node · price resilience where disruption value is highest · preserve concentration where scale is the advantage · re-evaluate criticality as tech/markets evolve
