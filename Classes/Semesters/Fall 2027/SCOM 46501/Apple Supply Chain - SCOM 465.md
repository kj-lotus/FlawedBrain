## Diagnosing Apple's Business Model

- **Customer value:** premium, tightly-integrated experience customers pay more for
- **Monetization:** devices + services + accessories reinforcing each other
- **Operating boundary:** what Apple owns/controls (design, chips) vs. outsources (assembly, many components)

## Discussion Answers (Q1–Q6)

### Q1 — Strategy + ecosystem

Apple's strategy: sell premium, tightly-integrated hardware that locks customers into a high-margin services ecosystem. iPhone = the anchor device — even as unit growth slows, it stays critical because it sustains the installed base that services revenue depends on.

### Q2 — Competitive advantage

- Design-manufacturing co-development (embedded engineering with suppliers, not just POs)
- Capacity pre-funding/prepayment — buys exclusivity/priority on constrained components
- Foxconn/Shenzhen assembly flexibility — extreme volume ramps in short windows
- Vertical control at chip level — reduces dependence on outside roadmaps

### Q3 — Value creation

- Margin capture via vertical integration + services attach (pull iPhone revenue share, services revenue/device)
- Working capital efficiency — lean/negative cash conversion cycle from JIT inventory + supplier payment terms (pull DIO/turns/CCC, compare to Walmart/Amazon)

### Q4 — Strategic fit as market matures

- **More efficient:** core iPhone assembly/logistics — growth is flattening, so focus on cost discipline and inventory turns
- **Stay responsive:** SKU/configuration forecasting, and the wearables/services supply chain — younger, less mature, different partners than iPhone chain

### Q5 — Risk + control

- **China:** don't abandon it, but build real second-source assembly capacity elsewhere as a hedge
- **Single sourcing:** fine only where it buys genuine performance/exclusivity edge; dual-source anything that's just convenience
- **Tech ownership:** keep internalizing components core to differentiation (chips, sensors); leave commodity components outsourced

### Q6 — Recommendation for Tim Cook

- **Preserve:** co-developed supplier relationships + China-anchored assembly base (the real moat)
- **Redesign:** meaningful second assembly hub outside China (India/Vietnam) — real volume, not token diversification
- **Build next:** a lighter, faster supply chain org specifically for wearables/services hardware
- **Trade-off:** near-term efficiency/margin cost (new suppliers take years to match iPhone-chain quality/cost) in exchange for long-term resilience
