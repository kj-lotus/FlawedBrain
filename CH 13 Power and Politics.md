## The Rise and Reckoning of Sam Altman at OpenAI

- **The abrupt firing and reinstatement of OpenAI CEO Sam Altman in 2023 highlighted a major organizational power struggle**, revealing deep tensions between aggressive innovation and cautious governance within the company.
- **OpenAI’s unique structure—a nonprofit board overseeing a for-profit subsidiary—created conflicting priorities**, with the board focused on long-term safety and ethical oversight, while Altman pushed for rapid growth and commercial success.
- **Employee collective action played a decisive role**, as 702 out of 770 employees threatened to resign unless Altman was reinstated, demonstrating the significant influence of internal alliances and stakeholder dependence over formal authority.
- **Microsoft, as a major investor, leveraged its influence to support Altman**, showing how external stakeholders can impact internal power dynamics.
- **The board’s lack of broad support and inability to defend its actions publicly led to a rapid reversal**, with Altman and his allies returning and the board members who ousted him being replaced or resigning.
- **The episode illustrates that power in organizations is not solely determined by formal authority**, but also by personal influence, stakeholder relationships, and the ability to mobilize support.
- **Power dynamics shape company culture, communication, collaboration, information flow, and productivity**, and are closely linked to leadership and political behavior within organizations.

## Power and Leadership

- **Power is the capacity, discretion, and means to enforce one’s will over others, and it is fundamentally based on dependence.** The more others rely on someone for resources or opportunities, the more powerful that person becomes; if alternatives increase, power decreases.
- **Power can be used ethically or unethically, and dependence can lead to exploitation.** Examples include executives or influential employees using their positions for personal gain or engaging in unethical behaviors like sexual harassment, which has significant negative impacts on individuals and organizations.
- **Money is a key source of dependence and power.** Organizations or individuals financially dependent on a single client or contract (such as government contracts) are vulnerable to shifts in power, especially during events like government shutdowns.
- **Key differences between power and leadership:**
    - Power does not require goal compatibility—only dependence—while leadership requires alignment between leader and follower goals.
    - Power primarily involves downward influence, whereas leadership values lateral and upward relationships as well.
    - Leadership emphasizes style and the process of decision-making and support, while power focuses on tactics to secure compliance.

## Leadership Power in the Remote and Hybrid Workplace

- **Remote and hybrid work environments have shifted power dynamics from physical presence to virtual influence**, where visibility and active participation on digital platforms (e-mail, video calls, collaboration tools) can increase informal power regardless of formal titles.
- **Individuals who frequently speak, take central roles, and receive affirming responses in videoconferencing are perceived as leaders**, gaining informal or personal power through their engagement and visibility.
- **Access to digital information flows and control over key documents or communication threads is a major source of power**, emphasizing network centrality over hierarchical authority and requiring leaders to create inclusive systems to avoid concentration of power among a few highly connected individuals.
- **Power arises from controlling resources or information valued by others**, and can be exercised in any context where dependence exists, not just in formal leadership roles.

## Bases of Power

- **Formal power is derived from an individual's position within an organization and includes three bases:** coercive power (the ability to punish or control), reward power (the ability to provide benefits or rewards), and legitimate power (the authority that comes from a formal position or role).
- **Personal power stems from an individual's unique characteristics and includes two bases:** expert power (influence based on skills or expertise) and referent power (influence based on identification, admiration, or respect).
- **Power originates from both formal organizational roles and personal attributes, with each base offering distinct ways to influence others.**

## Formal Power

- **Formal power is derived from an individual's organizational position** and can manifest as coercive, reward, or legitimate power.
- **Coercive power operates through fear of negative consequences** such as termination, demotion, undesirable assignments, or public embarrassment. It can be enforced by supervisors or algorithmic systems (e.g., electronic surveillance, opaque gig economy algorithms) and may involve withholding key information to induce compliance.
- **Reward power is based on the ability to provide positive benefits** like financial incentives (pay, raises, bonuses) or nonfinancial rewards (recognition, promotions, desirable assignments). Individuals may exercise both coercive and reward power, and the distribution of rewards can be influenced by factors like political ideology and organizational tenure, sometimes perpetuating inequalities.
- **Legitimate power stems from formal authority tied to a structural position** within the organization, leading others to comply due to the accepted hierarchy. This form of power is reinforced by visible organizational structures and is more readily accepted when the authority figure is recognized as high status or when subordinates are less experienced.
- **Legitimate power can mitigate the effects of coercive power**, as individuals with high legitimate power are less likely to face negative consequences when challenging unethical actions.

## Personal Power

- **Personal power arises from an individual's unique characteristics and is distinct from formal power; its two main bases are expertise and the respect and admiration of others.**
- **Expert power is derived from specialized skills or knowledge, making others dependent on the expert to achieve goals; however, overreliance on entrenched experts can hinder innovation and team performance, as experts may ignore feedback and fail to adapt to new advancements.** For example, Mark Zuckerberg's expertise in computer programming gives him expert power at Facebook.
- **Referent power is based on identification, admiration, and likability, allowing individuals to influence others even without formal authority; this is evident in celebrity endorsements and influencer marketing, where charismatic figures sway consumer choices.** The rise of virtual influencers like Lil Miquela demonstrates that referent power can be algorithmically designed, with studies showing customers often trust virtual influencers more than human ones, leading to higher purchase intentions and greater engagement in organizational training contexts.

## Which Bases of Power Are Most Effective?

- **The effectiveness of formal and personal power bases depends on how those subject to the power perceive and respond to the leader.** For example, dependents see angry leaders as having higher legitimate power, which increases their loyalty and perception of leader effectiveness.
- **Coercive power and low referent power are generally seen as ineffective.** Leaders who rely on coercion or lack referent power tend to lose loyalty from followers and may even provoke deviant behaviors against them.
- **Referent power is a particularly strong motivator.** When leaders or spokespeople are admired or respected (high referent power), they can significantly influence others, as seen in marketing strategies that pair celebrities with brands to boost appeal and reach large audiences.
- **Modern approaches to leveraging referent power include creating engaging content and partnerships with influential figures.** For example, online series featuring celebrities with strong referent power can effectively promote products and shape culture, reaching millions of people.

## Dependence: The Key to Power

- **Power is fundamentally based on dependence**, meaning that one party's power over another increases as the other party's dependence on them grows.
- **Dependence arises when someone controls resources or outcomes that another person values**, such as information, money, or support.
- **The degree of power is determined by the level of dependence**—the more you need what someone else controls, the more power they have over you.

## The General Dependence Postulate

- **Power is based on dependence:** The more someone (B) depends on another (A), the more power A has over B.
- **Control of scarce resources increases power:** If you have something others need and you alone control it, you gain power over them due to their dependence.
- **Abundance reduces power:** Having something that is plentiful does not increase your power, as others can easily obtain it elsewhere.
- **Expanding options reduces others' power over you:** By increasing your own alternatives, you decrease your dependence and limit the power others have over you.
- **Organizations diversify suppliers to reduce dependence:** Companies often use multiple suppliers to avoid being overly dependent on a single source, thereby maintaining more control and reducing others' power over them.

## What Creates Dependence?

- **Dependence on a resource increases when it is important, scarce, and nonsubstitutable.**
- **Importance:** The more essential a resource is—whether for survival, convenience, or relationships—the greater the dependence on whoever controls it. Organizations reduce risk by ensuring multiple people manage key relationships, preventing overreliance on a single individual.
- **Scarcity:** When a resource (such as labor) is in short supply compared to demand, those who control it gain bargaining power. For example, medical professionals have more negotiating power and job security due to high demand and limited supply, while photographers face less power because their skills are more widely available.
- **Nonsubstitutability:** The fewer substitutes available for a resource, the more power its controller has. Steinway pianos once held unique market power due to their distinctiveness, but lost it when Yamaha produced high-quality alternatives. In the workforce, advancements in AI and robotics are making some skills more substitutable, reducing the power of those who possess them. However, new nonsubstitutable jobs may emerge as technology evolves.

## Formal Small-Group Networks

- **Three common small-group communication networks are chain, wheel, and all-channel.** The chain follows a strict hierarchy, the wheel centers communication through one leader, and the all-channel allows free communication among all members.
- **Each network structure has different strengths:** the chain is best for accuracy, the wheel facilitates leadership emergence and central control, and the all-channel maximizes member satisfaction and open contribution.
- **Informal networks exist alongside formal ones and can significantly impact power, influence, and coordination within organizations.** Even with open structures, actual communication depends on trust and willingness to share, and dense informal networks can disrupt innovation and relationship-building.

## Social Network Analysis: A Tool for Assessing Resource Dependence

- **Social network analysis** is used to examine communication patterns and resource exchanges within organizations, identifying how information actually flows among members.
- In a social network, each person or group is a **node**, and the connections between them are called **ties**; strong ties indicate frequent communication or resource exchange.
- **Brokers** are nodes that connect otherwise unconnected nodes, facilitating resource flows and often holding more power due to their unique position.
- A **sociogram** visually represents the real flow of resources and information, differing from a formal organization chart that shows official authority lines.
- Brokers gain power because others depend on them for access to resources, but they may overlook opportunities to use this power due to limited or unreliable information about potential new connections.
- Personality traits such as **insensitivity to rewards**, **extraversion**, and **dark triad traits** are linked to higher power and central positions in social networks.
- **Stereotype threat** can negatively impact women in brokerage roles, as the assertiveness required may conflict with gender stereotypes, leading to anxiety and fear of negative evaluation.
- Relationships within networks, such as friendships and partnerships, foster mutual reliance and obligation, which are crucial for both organizational effectiveness and employee well-being; losing these ties increases the likelihood of employee turnover.
- Organizations can implement social network analysis by tracking communication flows (e.g., emails, document sharing) using big data and AI, or by analyzing HR data to map interactions and identify influential brokers.
- Leaders use sociograms and network data to recognize key brokers, helping them manage influence, gain support, and balance power within the organization.

## Influence Tactics

- **Influence tactics are methods leaders use to translate power into action and guide others toward goals.**
- There are nine main influence tactics: legitimacy, rational persuasion, inspirational appeals, consultation, exchange, personal appeals, ingratiation, pressure, and coalitions.
- **Legitimacy** involves using authority or organizational rules to justify requests.
- **Rational persuasion** uses logical arguments and factual evidence to show a request is reasonable.
- **Inspirational appeals** target emotions by connecting requests to values, needs, hopes, or aspirations.
- **Consultation** seeks support by involving others in planning or decision-making.
- **Exchange** offers benefits or favors in return for compliance.
- **Personal appeals** rely on friendship or loyalty to gain agreement.
- **Ingratiation** uses flattery or friendly behavior before making a request.
- **Pressure** applies threats, repeated demands, or warnings to influence others.
- **Coalitions** gather support from others to strengthen the influence on the target.

## Using Influence Tactics

- **Rational persuasion, inspirational appeals, and consultation are the most effective tactics for influencing workplace performance**, with rational persuasion being especially effective when supported by data and clear evidence of value.
- **Inspirational appeals and consultation are particularly strong for building work relationships**, while rational persuasion is effective but not as impactful in this area.
- **Pressure tactics are generally the least effective and can backfire**, damaging relationships and reducing influence.
- **Ingratiation can improve relational outcomes, especially in routine situations or when the audience is indifferent to the outcome**, but its effectiveness is limited by context.
- **Rational persuasion is effective for both upward (influencing supervisors) and downward (influencing subordinates) influence**, but it is stronger for downward influence; inspirational appeals work best downward, and ingratiation is most effective laterally or downward.
- **Demonstrations and prototypes, when combined with persuasive tactics, significantly increase the likelihood of convincing supervisors**, as shown in creative industries.
- **The effectiveness of influence tactics depends on the direction of influence, the audience, and factors like tactic sequencing, skill, and organizational culture**.
- **Starting with “soft” tactics (personal/inspirational appeals, rational persuasion, consultation) is generally more effective**, and only moving to “hard” tactics (exchange, coalitions) if necessary, as these carry higher risks and costs.
- **Individuals more likely to comply with soft tactics are reflective, intrinsically motivated, and have high self-esteem**, while those who respond to hard tactics are action-oriented, extrinsically motivated, and prioritize getting along with others.

## Political Skill and the Use of Influence Tactics

- **Political skill is the ability to influence others to achieve personal objectives**, relying on understanding others’ demands, resources, and preferences, and recognizing and capitalizing on opportunities.
- **Political skill is especially valuable in jobs requiring social skills**, such as sales consultants and real estate agents, where influence can be exerted subtly without detection.
- **Organizational culture shapes which influence tactics are acceptable and who is influential**; extraverts thrive in team-oriented cultures, while conscientious individuals excel in technical, independent environments.
- **Motivation and personality traits impact the development and use of political skill**; extraverts, motivated, risk-tolerant, and Machiavellian individuals are more likely to use political influence, with Machiavellians sometimes using political skill for self-interested or destructive purposes.
- **Political skill leads to positive outcomes**, including increased self-efficacy, job satisfaction, organizational commitment, reduced stress, improved performance, more organizational citizenship behaviors, enhanced reputation, higher income, more prestigious positions, and greater career satisfaction.

## How Power Affects People

- **Power can corrupt by increasing self-interest and self-serving behavior.** When you hold power, you are more likely to prioritize your own needs and goals over those of others, as power liberates you to focus inward.
- **Power leads to objectification of others and peripheral relationships.** You may start seeing others as tools to achieve your goals, making relationships less central to your decision-making.
- **Powerholders react strongly to threats and seek to retain power, sometimes at others’ expense.** If your power is threatened, you are more likely to take actions to keep it, even if those actions harm others.
- **Formal power can distort perception and emotional understanding.** You may become less effective at interpreting others’ emotions, such as being quicker to notice anger because it signals a threat to your power.
- **Power increases the likelihood of self-interested decisions in moral dilemmas.** For example, those in power, like hedge fund managers, may take greater risks with others’ resources because they benefit from gains but are less affected by losses.
- **Power can lead to overconfidence and denigration of others.** You may become more certain in your decisions and more willing to put others down.

## Power Dynamics

- **Power's effects are complex and not always negative; whether power corrupts depends on both personal and environmental factors.**
- Individuals with collectivist values and strong moral identities are less likely to be corrupted by power, while those with a strong need for power or dark personality traits (like Machiavellianism) are more susceptible to corruption.
- **Environmental factors such as group identification and clear accountability reduce the likelihood of power corruption.**
- Power is not always permanent; it can be maintained or lost based on personal strategies, influence techniques, and follower characteristics.
- Attribution theory helps explain why followers may continue to support powerholders, as it shapes how they interpret leaders' behaviors.
- **External factors like competition and the abilities of rivals can lead to the loss of power, regardless of the powerholder's actions.**
- Powerholders often lose power due to their own ethical failures, inability to recognize threats, or biased decision-making, which can provoke public outrage or organizational failure.

## Sexual Harassment: Unequal Power in the Workplace

- **Sexual harassment involves any unwanted sexual activity that affects employment or creates a hostile work environment**, including unwelcome advances, requests for favors, and verbal or physical harassment that disrupts work performance or creates an intimidating, hostile, or offensive environment.
- **Women are more likely to experience sexual harassment, but men and non-binary individuals are also affected**, with many incidents going unreported due to stigma and social expectations; over one-third of victims do not report harassment.
- **Consequences of sexual harassment include decreased employee loyalty, lowered productivity, diminished motivation, and severe mental health impacts** such as anxiety, depression, and PTSD, with only 10% of victims reporting good mental health.
- **Legal protections exist in 142 nations, but gaps remain in 190 countries**, and implementation of policies is often lacking; movements like #MeToo have shifted attitudes, increasing accountability for offenders.
- **Power dynamics are central to understanding sexual harassment**, especially in relationships with large power differentials, such as supervisor-employee, where supervisors control important resources and may abuse their power if controls are lacking.
- **Managers must actively prevent and address sexual harassment by defining policies, protecting whistleblowers, investigating complaints, disciplining offenders, and providing training**, as both managers and organizations can be held liable if they fail to act or are unaware of harassment.

## Politics: Power in Action

- **Power is always present in group settings**, as individuals seek to influence others, gain rewards, and progress in their careers.
- **Organizational politics occur when individuals use their power to take action**, aiming to achieve personal or group objectives.
- **Political skill enhances effectiveness**, allowing individuals to use their power bases more successfully within the organization.

## Political Behavior

- **Organizational politics involves the use of power to influence decision making**, often for self-serving purposes and sometimes in ways not officially sanctioned by the organization.
- **Political behavior consists of actions outside formal job requirements** that aim to affect the distribution of advantages and disadvantages within the organization.
- **Political behavior utilizes various power bases**—coercive, reward, expert, legitimate, and referent—to influence organizational goals, criteria, or decision-making processes.
- **Examples of political behavior include** withholding information, forming coalitions, whistleblowing, spreading rumors, leaking confidential information, exchanging favors, and lobbying for or against individuals or decisions.
- **Political behavior can provide individuals with a sense of empowerment and control**, especially in highly political environments, but it can also be exhausting.
- **While often viewed negatively, political behavior is not always harmful** and can sometimes serve positive or necessary functions within organizations.

## The Reality of Politics

- **People interpret organizational politics in different ways:** Some see it as destructive and manipulative, others as a necessary evil, some as a strategic tool for achieving goals, and some as an integrated part of decision making.
- **Managers generally view political behavior as a significant and unavoidable aspect of organizational life:** Many believe that using political behavior ethically (without directly harming others) is necessary to accomplish tasks.
- **Politics arise because organizations consist of individuals and groups with differing values, goals, and interests:** This diversity leads to competition over limited resources like budgets, workspace, and compensation.
- **Resource scarcity intensifies political behavior:** When resources are limited, not all interests can be satisfied, and gains by one group are often perceived as losses by another, increasing competition and political maneuvering.
- **Ambiguity in decision-making fuels politics:** Many organizational decisions are based on subjective interpretations (e.g., what counts as good performance), creating a space where influence and political behavior thrive.
- **Even those reluctant to engage in politics may do so if it serves prosocial goals or helps others:** The context and perceived purpose of political behavior can affect individuals’ willingness to participate.
- **Political actions are often interpreted differently depending on perspective:** What one person sees as selfless organizational benefit, another may view as self-serving behavior.

## Gossip and the Grapevine

- **The grapevine is the informal communication network within groups or organizations, distinct from the negative connotations of gossip or rumors.**
- **Untrustworthy gossip can lead to unfair judgments and dislike, but the grapevine overall creates social pressure that can improve performance, while also potentially harming employee well-being.**
- **Rumors and gossip transmitted through the grapevine play roles in sharing information, exerting political influence, and navigating organizational hierarchies.**
- **Word-of-mouth information from peers significantly influences job applicants’ decisions to join organizations, sometimes more than online company ratings.**
- **Employers can use the grapevine to recruit trusted candidates from employees’ personal networks, as seen in companies like TWT Group.**
- **Managers can analyze gossip and small talk within employee networks to understand information flow and identify influencers—individuals who are highly connected and trusted.**
- **Small talkers, who regularly engage in casual conversation, are often influencers and are more likely to keep their jobs during layoffs.**
- **Follow-up questions during conversations predict greater liking and intentions to continue interacting, highlighting the importance of active engagement.**
- **Influencers who collaborate and help others, rather than just gossip, are more likely to hold central positions in the network.**
- **Being an active, helpful participant in the grapevine is generally beneficial, even though the grapevine is not officially controlled by the organization.**

## The Causes and Consequences of Political Behavior

- **Political behavior in organizations arises from the constant presence of differing interests, limited resources, and ambiguity in decision-making.**
- **Consequences of political behavior can include both positive and negative outcomes, such as increased personal or group power, but also decreased trust, reduced job satisfaction, and potential conflict.**
- **Ethical considerations are central to political behavior, as actions can range from legitimate influence to manipulation or unethical conduct, impacting organizational culture and relationships.**

## Factors Contributing to Political Behavior

- **Political behavior in organizations varies due to both individual and organizational factors.**
- **Individual factors** that encourage political behavior include personality traits such as high self-monitoring, internal locus of control, and a high need for power. Individuals with Machiavellian tendencies are especially likely to use politics for personal gain.
- Employees who are highly invested in the organization and have much to lose are less likely to use illegitimate political means, while those with more external job opportunities or scarce skills are more likely to engage in political behavior.
- Individuals with strong political skills—such as reading social cues, adapting behavior to situations, and networking—are more likely to engage in and be rewarded for political behavior.
- **Organizational factors** that promote political behavior include declining or changing resources, opportunities for promotion, and significant resource reallocations, all of which increase competition and conflict.
- Cultures with low trust, role ambiguity, unclear performance evaluations, democratic decision making, high performance pressure, and self-serving leadership foster more political activity.
- Greater role ambiguity allows employees to engage in political behavior with less oversight, as expectations for their roles are unclear.
- Organizations that use a zero-sum or win–lose approach to rewards (where one person’s gain is another’s loss) motivate employees to engage in political behavior, often leading to actions that undermine others to increase personal visibility and rewards.

## Why People Acquiesce to Political Behavior

- **Political behavior is inherently interpersonal**, involving attempts by individuals to influence others' actions.
- **Cialdini’s Seven Principles of Persuasion** are key social forces that drive people to comply with influence attempts:
    - **Reciprocity**: You feel compelled to return favors or kindnesses received from others.
    - **Consistency/Commitment**: You are motivated to act in ways that align with your previous decisions or commitments.
    - **Social Proof**: You look to others’ actions and behaviors to guide your own, especially in uncertain situations.
    - **Liking**: You are more likely to be influenced by people you like or have positive feelings toward.
    - **Authority**: You tend to comply with requests from those you perceive as having power or expertise.
    - **Scarcity**: You place higher value on things that are rare or becoming less available.
    - **Unity**: You are most influenced by people with whom you share a sense of identity or belonging.
- These principles are universal social forces that significantly impact behavior within organizations.

## How Do People Respond to Organizational Politics?

- **Organizational politics often result in negative outcomes** such as decreased job satisfaction, increased anxiety and stress, higher turnover, and reduced performance.
- The more prevalent politics are in a workplace, the more likely employees are to experience negative effects, regardless of whether politics are seen as necessary.
- **Perceptions of unfairness in political environments can demotivate employees** and lead to self-reported declines in performance.
- **Understanding the decision-making process moderates the impact of politics:** When employees understand who makes decisions and why, they may view politics as an opportunity and perform better; when understanding is low, politics are seen as a threat, harming performance.
- **Defensive behaviors are common responses to perceived threats from organizational politics or change.** These include:
    - Avoiding action (e.g., strictly interpreting rules, transferring responsibility, feigning ignorance, prolonging tasks, or appearing supportive without real action)
    - Avoiding blame (e.g., excessive documentation, evading risky situations, making excuses, blaming external factors, manipulating information)
    - Avoiding change (e.g., resisting change or protecting self-interest by withholding information)
- **Defensive behaviors may offer short-term protection but are ultimately draining and counterproductive** for employees in the long run.

## Myth or Science?

- **Office politics are often unavoidable and influence team dynamics and work outcomes**, regardless of whether you choose to participate.
- **Negative or "bad" politics, such as spreading rumors or self-advancement at others' expense, are harmful and should be avoided** because they decrease engagement, productivity, and retention.
- **Not all office politics are negative; "good" politics can help you advance your interests, gain recognition, influence others, and contribute to important decisions** without causing harm.
- **Developing political skill in four key areas—emotional intelligence, interpersonal influence, networking ability, and apparent sincerity—improves leadership, promotion prospects, and performance**.
- **Political skill benefits even those who are less extraverted or not the most knowledgeable**, making it a valuable asset for career advancement.
- **Completely avoiding office politics can have negative consequences**, such as lower employee engagement, especially under managers lacking political skill.
- **Cultivating positive political skills is more effective than avoiding office politics**, as it benefits your career, your team, and your organization.

## Voice and Silence

- **Voice is discretionary communication of suggestions, concerns, or opinions about work-related issues to those who can act, and is a form of influence that can challenge the status quo, support others, or be defensive/destructive.** Voice contributes to a fair and equitable work environment and is influenced by self-evaluation, initiative, responsibility, engagement, workplace climate, and supervisor behavior. Extraversion, openness, and conscientiousness (Big Five traits) increase likelihood of voice.
- **Encouraging employee voice improves organizational performance and emotional outcomes.** Leadership and managerial practices that support voice lead to higher job performance and positive emotions. Manager responses are crucial: angry or dismissive reactions reduce psychological safety and willingness to speak up, while clear explanations for not acting on input maintain fairness and safety.
- **Silence, the withholding of communication, is also a form of influence and is shaped by individual differences, motivations, message content, delivery style, and perceptions of openness and trustworthiness.** Fear, powerlessness, and negative emotions increase silence. Environments should empower sharing and avoid rewarding silence or punishing voice. Silence is common and problematic, often resulting from interpersonal dynamics, impression management, and unmet needs, and can lead to discomfort when people feel obligated but unable to speak up.

## Impression Management (IM)

- **Impression management (IM) is the process by which you attempt to control how others perceive and evaluate you, especially in organizational settings.** Positive impressions can lead to job offers, favorable evaluations, salary increases, and promotions.
- **IM techniques include ingratiation (conformity and favors), defensive tactics (excuses and apologies), self-focused strategies (self-promotion and enhancement), and assertive tactics (flattery and exemplification).** For example, agreeing with someone to gain approval (conformity), doing favors, making excuses for mistakes, apologizing, highlighting your achievements, exaggerating the value of your work, complimenting others, and going above and beyond to appear dedicated.
- **IM can be conscious or unconscious, and its effectiveness depends on whether it is perceived as authentic or disingenuous.** Authentic IM (such as modest self-disclosure) tends to improve relationships and perceptions, while inauthentic IM can lead to negative outcomes like reduced performance, increased anxiety, and negative feelings.
- **IM is common in organizations but can have negative consequences for those who use it, especially leaders and managers.** For example, ingratiating behavior toward superiors can lead to resentment and undermining, and leaders’ IM attempts may be viewed positively by some audiences but negatively by others.
- **Cultural adaptation is important for IM, especially for expatriates representing organizations in different countries, as norms and expectations for IM can vary.**

## An Ethical Choice

- **First impressions in interviews are crucial**, as people form opinions about your personality and intelligence within the first 30 seconds.
- **Using impression management (IM) tactics—such as dressing professionally, highlighting accomplishments, expressing interest, and using positive nonverbal cues like smiling and nodding—can significantly improve your chances of being hired.**
- **Applicants who confidently discuss their qualifications and speak positively about the organization are perceived as better fits for both the job and the company.**
- **Positive nonverbal behaviors not only enhance your image but also improve the interviewer’s mood, which can lead to higher applicant ratings.**
- **The ability to manage impressions in interviews is a good predictor of your ability to do so on the job.**
- **Overusing IM tactics or misrepresenting your qualifications can backfire, potentially leading to termination or creating unrealistic expectations that harm your reputation if you cannot deliver.**
- **An ethical and effective interview strategy involves presenting your skills and enthusiasm truthfully, without exaggeration, and being honest about areas where you lack experience while showing a willingness to learn.**
- **Honest impression management leads to greater interview success and ensures a better long-term fit and satisfaction for both you and the employer.**

## Mapping Your Political Career

- **Hard work alone is not enough for career advancement**—your peers are also working hard, so you need to consider other strategies to stand out.
- **Understanding power and politics is crucial for your career**; you should identify your ambitions, who has the power to help you achieve them, and your relationships with these key individuals.
- **Creating a political map helps you visualize your connections** with influential people who impact your career progression, highlighting where you have strong, weak, or no connections.
- **Strengthening your network is essential**; if you lack direct influence with decision-makers, you can use indirect connections (such as mutual friends or colleagues) to build relationships and increase your influence.
- **Not all connections have equal power**, so you need to assess the influence of each person on your map and prioritize building relationships accordingly.
- **Navigating organizational politics is a normal part of professional life**, and being strategic about your relationships can give you a competitive edge for promotions and opportunities.
